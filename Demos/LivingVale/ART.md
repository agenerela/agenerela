# Living Vale — art kit

**Status:** art pilot (G7a), 5 November 2026. One candidate style, built to be judged in the
game. The style decision belongs to the lead (`docs/demos/living-vale-assets.md`).

## Provenance

Every model and the palette texture in `Assets/LivingVale/Art/` is **procedurally generated by
our own scripts** in [`Art~/blender/`](Art~/blender/), with Blender's Python API (plain `bpy`
and `bmesh`, no add-ons). No third-party models, textures, scans or asset packs are used, and no
generative model made any of it, so nothing here belongs in a `GENERATED.md`. The scripts are
MIT like the rest of the repository, and so is what they produce.

The `~` on `Art~/` keeps Unity from importing the scripts, the manifest and the previews.

## Rebuild the kit

From the repository root, with Blender 4.x or 5.x (made with 5.2.2):

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python Demos/LivingVale/Art~/blender/build_kit.py -- --render .scratch/lv-g7/renders
```

It writes `Assets/LivingVale/Art/Textures/LV_Palette.png`, one
`Assets/LivingVale/Art/Models/<id>.fbx` per asset and `Art~/kit_manifest.json` (triangles and
Unity-space bounds per asset), then renders contact sheets and per-row views into the folder
after `--render` (Workbench, no GPU needed; backface culling is on, so an inverted face shows as
a hole). `--only id,id` rebuilds just those assets. It takes about ten seconds.

Then let Unity pick the files up: open the project, or run **Living Vale → Art → Set Up Art Kit**
(batch: `-executeMethod LivingVale.Editor.ArtKit.SetUp`), which creates the palette material if
it is missing and reimports the models. Keep each FBX's path: its `.meta` holds the GUID that
scenes reference. The FBX exporter stamps a creation time, so a rebuild changes every file even
when the geometry is the same; commit only the models you meant to change.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `Art~/blender/palette.py` | The 64 colours and their cells. Append, never reorder |
| `Art~/blender/kitlib.py` | `Kit`: boxes, cylinders, hulls, limbs and rings into one bmesh, colour per face |
| `Art~/blender/assets.py` | Every asset as a function, the export list `ASSETS` and the render rows |
| `Art~/blender/build_kit.py` | Palette, export, manifest, renders |

## Style rules

- **Low-poly, flat-shaded, readable at gameplay distance.** Shapes are boxes, prisms and hulls;
  detail is a silhouette or a colour change, never a texture.
- **One palette, one material.** Every face's UVs sit on the centre of one 8×8-pixel cell of
  `LV_Palette.png` (64×64, point filtered, no mipmaps, uncompressed), and every model uses
  `Assets/LivingVale/Art/Materials/LV_Palette.mat` (URP Lit, smoothness 0). A new colour is a
  new cell at the end of `palette.py`.
- **Grounded medieval colours:** weathered stone, aged timber, thatch and slate, undyed and
  plant-dyed cloth. Vale blue is the one saturated accent (the player, Mara's canopy);
  Ashwatch's red and gold mark the castle and its guards.
- **Real sizes in metres.** A person is 1.8 m, a door 1.9 m, a cottage wall 2.2–2.5 m.
- **Pivot at the base centre, front facing Unity +Z.** In Blender the front faces −Y with Z up.
  Unity applies the axes the FBX declares, so the exporter's `axis_forward` cannot turn a model
  round: `build_kit.py` turns each mesh half a turn about Z just for the export, which lands the
  front on Unity's +Z with an identity root rotation and unit scale (`FBX_SCALE_ALL`,
  `bake_space_transform`). A test checks the facing on the characters, the wolf and the
  cottages. The sword is the one exception to the pivot rule: its pivot is the grip, blade along
  +Y, ready for a hand socket.
- **Characters stand relaxed, arms down, not in a T-pose.** One body; outfits change colour and
  add one accent each (cloak, apron, robe, hood and scarf, helmet and pauldrons), the
  "recognisable silhouettes and outfit accents" of design §7.

## Using it from a scene builder

`ArtKit` (in `Assets/LivingVale/Editor/Art/`, the Editor assembly) is the only door. It is
editor-only on purpose: the builders run at edit time and save prefab instances into the scene,
so nothing needs `Resources/` or loading at runtime.

```csharp
// A model, or null when it is missing: keep the primitive as the fallback.
GameObject cottage = ArtKit.Place("cottage_a", new Vector3(-10f, 0f, 6f), 180f, parent)
                     ?? Primitive("West cottage", PrimitiveType.Cube, ...);

// An NPC's capsule body becomes a humanoid; the capsule keeps its collider for sight rays.
GameObject body = ArtKit.SwapBody(capsule, ArtOutfit.Villager);   // null: capsule untouched
Renderer flash = body != null ? body.GetComponent<Renderer>() : capsule.GetComponent<Renderer>();
```

- `Place(id, position, yRotation = 0, parent = null)` instantiates the linked model with its
  front along the rotated +Z. A building's door is on its front, so turn it to face the street.
- `SwapBody(capsule, outfit)` puts the humanoid at the capsule's feet under the same parent, on
  the same layer, and strips the capsule's mesh renderer while keeping its collider. Call it
  before handing renderers to `CombatBody`.
- `ArtKit.Entries` lists every id with its category, collider flag and height; `ArtKit.Ids` is
  what the tests check resolves.
- **Art Preview:** **Living Vale → Art → Build Art Preview Scene** writes
  `Assets/LivingVale/Scenes/ArtPreview.unity` (not in the build), every asset in labelled rows;
  **Capture Art Preview** renders it to `.scratch/lv-g7/unity-renders/`.

## In the game (G7b)

The Village scene's builders now dress their primitives with the kit through `ArtDressing`
(`Assets/LivingVale/Editor/Art/`). One rule keeps gameplay where it was: **collision belongs to
the primitive, looks belong to the kit.**

- `ArtDressing.Dress(primitive, id, position, yaw, scale)` (and `DressAll` for a group) shows the
  kit model, hides the primitive's mesh and keeps its collider exactly where it was; the model's
  own mesh colliders are switched off. Walls, talk ranges, sight lines and combat reach therefore
  cannot move when the art changes. A missing model leaves the primitive untouched.
- `Attach(parent, id, localPosition, localRotation, layer)` puts moving parts (the raider's and
  the player's sword, the wolf, the quest wreck and herbs) under their parent, with no collider.
- `Decorate(parent, id, position, ...)` adds scenery with no primitive behind it: market clutter,
  wild feverleaf and signposts with no collider, and the trees and rocks scattered along the paths
  and near the cliffs with their mesh colliders. Those are seeded (the same rebuild plants the same
  forest) and kept off every path, out of every place's radius plus 10 m, off the river and away
  from the castle.
- Bodies: the player, the eight NPCs and the raiders go through `ArtKit.SwapBody`, outfits by role
  (`ArtDressing.OutfitFor`: Iven healer, Brann and Elra guard, Kest raider, the rest villager); the
  capsules keep their colliders. The wolf model replaces the wolf's two cubes. `CombatBody` gets
  the humanoid's or the wolf's renderer for the hit flash.
- **Living Vale → Art → Capture Village Scene Shots**
  (`-executeMethod LivingVale.Editor.VillageSceneShots.Capture`, not `-nographics`) renders the
  built scene from eight fixed viewpoints into `.scratch/lv-g7/scene-shots/`.

### G7c: ground, mountains, cliffs and patients

- Briar Glen's ground is packed earth (`GlenEarth`) instead of grey stone, with four `cobbles`
  patches over the square; Ashwatch's forecourt is cobbled too. Cobbles have no colliders and are
  8 cm high, so nobody's footing changes.
- The 28 distant egg-shaped mountains are `mountain_a` and `mountain_b`, scaled to each egg's size.
- The four boundary walls are lined with `cliff` modules facing inward, their faces on the walls'
  inner faces, and each of the 48 cliff crests carries a stretched module. The walls and crests keep
  their colliders.
- Iven's three porch patients sit on their benches as `humanoid_patient_seated` (cream tunic, grey
  shawl and blanket). `QuestWorldView` still hides them when the healer gets the parcel.

Notes for the swap:

- `CombatBody`'s hit flash used to lerp `_BaseColor` toward near-white, which does not show on
  the palette material (its base colour is already white). Since G7b it lerps toward a warm red,
  and since G7c toward a stronger red, for 0.2 s instead of 0.12 s. An emission pulse was tried
  and dropped: URP's material validation turns the palette material's `_EMISSION` keyword off again
  on the next editor start, and a property block cannot turn it on.
- The kit's own mesh colliders are switched off wherever a model dresses a primitive; only the
  scattered trees and rocks keep theirs. Where they apply, trees collide on the canopy too, which
  the player cannot reach.
- A model has no LODs yet; at these triangle counts the whole village is a few tens of thousands
  of triangles.

## Not done yet

- **Rig and animation.** The humanoid and wolf are static meshes. The next step is one shared
  humanoid skeleton (idle, walk, attack, block, stagger, incapacitated), the asset plan's step 6.
- **The style decision.** This is one candidate, judged from Blender and Unity renders only. The
  lead should look at it in the lit village from chat and gameplay distance before it becomes
  the style.
- The region's other places (Reedbank, Ashwatch's interior) need more modules: a castle tower,
  an inn, a smithy, bridges.
